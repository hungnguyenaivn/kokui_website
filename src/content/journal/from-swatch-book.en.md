Some afternoons, the kokui worktable disappears under dozens of swatch books. Each comes from a Japanese mill, and each page holds a small pinked sample with its code, composition and weight.

## Touch first, look second

We don't choose cloth on a screen. A swatch only really speaks when you pick it up: how it stands when folded, how it softens when crumpled, whether the reverse shows its white weft.

Then we take it to the window. In daylight, charcoal sometimes leans blue and indigo sometimes reads almost black. Those are the tones a pair will carry onto the street.

## Choose very few

After each session only a handful of samples stay. We look for cloth heavy enough to hold a wide leg, soft enough to wear all day, and alive enough to change with the person wearing it.

Names like **Charcoal**, **Black Prince** and **Indigo** each began on a page like that.
